# Going Live

For me, making a Git repository public feels a lot like publishing my books.

You spend months building, testing, rewriting, breaking things, and fixing them again. Eventually there you release it into the world and hope others see the same value in it as you do.

Building [Scandinavian Hacking](https://scandinavianhacking.se/) from scratch has been a great journey. I've learned a lot, met a lot of great people, and had opportunities I would never have expected a few years ago.

My presentation at Lindholmen Software Development Day 2026 is a good example. It was an experience that would never have happened without all the things running a business has forced me to learn.

## BASM

[BASM](https://github.com/The66Monkey/BASM) is, in many ways, the culmination of both Scandinavian Hacking's growth into a full business and a crystallization of ideas that have guided me for years.

Like many complex systems, my business infrastructure evolved gradually. New services were added when I needed them, old ones were replaced, configurations accumulated, and over time the whole thing became increasingly difficult to reproduce from scratch. Everything worked, but only because years of decisions and adjustments were buried inside the system.

When it came time to move to new hardware, I found myself facing a familiar problem. Something similar to a software rewrite in spirit. I needed a way to redeploy the entire business environment without manually coneecting every system again.

Part of the inspiration came from the LAMP stack. Not any particular implementation, but the idea itself. LAMP is beloved framework because it combined a number of proven technologies into a package that people understood. The individual components can be swaped, but it gives you a core understanindg of the product of a whole from the induvidual components.

Rebuilding the environment also forced me to re-evaluate many of the tools I was using. A failure in my time-tracking system highlighted the importance of proper record keeping. Other decisions, such as container platforms, backup strategies, and business applications, had to be weighed against both practical requirements and the realities of accounting and record-retention regulations.

Spending years experimenting with different tools has been valuable for me because infrastructure is part of my business. For most business owners, it is not. A consultant, carpenter, designer, or accountant should not have to become an expert in mail servers, reverse proxies, backups, and container orchestration just to run their company.

My hope is that BASM helps lower that barrier and allows people to spend more time on the work they actually set out to do.
